# PANDORA: PERMANENT DEATH ROLEPLAY
## Game Design Document (GDD)

---

# 1. Game Overview

### 1.1 Concept

The game is a closed-community Permanent Death Roleplay (PD) experience set within an Avatar-inspired world. Players participate in an ongoing conflict between the Resources Development Administration (RDA) and the Na'vi, with the possibility of additional independent affiliations such as rogues and lone warriors.

Unlike conventional Roblox PvP games, the experience is built around community-driven roleplay, faction warfare, resource management, character progression and persistent narrative development.

Players are expected to develop their characters through participation in faction activities, completing Trials, gathering resources, crafting equipment, constructing facilities and contributing to major community events.

The central gameplay experience revolves around organised PD events. These events provide opportunities for players to interact with the world, establish relationships, develop their factions, engage in combat and influence the ongoing narrative. Character deaths have permanent consequences, making decisions and engagements meaningful.

### 1.2 Core Design Pillars

**Permanent Consequences**

Characters can permanently die during officially recognised PD events. Death results in the loss of character-specific progression, equipment and possessions, with weapon skins being the only permanent exception.

**Community-Driven Narrative**

The community contributes to the ongoing story through roleplay, faction operations, political decisions, conflict and individual character development.

**Faction Identity**

The RDA and Na'vi have distinct organisational structures, objectives, resources, equipment and approaches to warfare.

**Meaningful Progression**

Faction advancement and weapon access are earned through mandatory, sequential Trials overseen exclusively by the Loreteam.

**Player-Driven Economy**

Players gather, craft, acquire, trade and potentially lose equipment through combat and Permanent Death.

**Loreteam Oversight**

The Loreteam maintains narrative continuity and controls faction progression, Trials, ranks and weapon kit assignments.

**Movement**

Movement is a key factor of the experience. Sliding, wall climbing, mounting and other traversal mechanics are core aspects of how players explore Pandora, engage in combat, pursue or escape opponents and carry out faction operations (see Section 6A).

# 2. Factions and Organisations

## 2.1 Resources Development Administration (RDA)

The RDA represents the human industrial and military presence on Pandora. Its primary objectives involve resource extraction, territorial expansion, scientific research and maintaining operational control over its facilities.

The RDA relies on organised military structures, advanced technology, logistical networks and industrial infrastructure.

### RDA Divisions

**Command**

Responsible for overall faction leadership, strategic decisions, military objectives and internal organisation.

**Military**

Responsible for combat operations, patrols, territorial defence, offensive campaigns and protection of RDA personnel.

**Research and Development**

Responsible for scientific research, technological development, experimentation and approved research-related activities.

**Engineering**

Responsible for constructing, maintaining and repairing RDA facilities, machinery, defensive structures and infrastructure.

**Logistics**

Responsible for resource transportation, storage, supply distribution and maintaining operational readiness.

**Medical**

Responsible for treating injured personnel, operating medical facilities and supporting military operations.

## 2.2 Na'vi

The Na'vi represent the indigenous population of Pandora. Their society is structured around clans, traditions, spiritual beliefs and their relationship with the natural environment.

Their objectives generally involve protecting their ancestral territories, preserving their culture, maintaining their relationship with Eywa and resisting human expansion.

### Na'vi Roles

**Olo'eyktan**

The clan leader responsible for political authority, strategic decisions and the overall direction of the clan.

**Tsahìk**

The spiritual leader responsible for cultural traditions, ceremonies, spiritual guidance and matters concerning Eywa.

**Hunters**

Responsible for hunting, gathering food, tracking wildlife and supplying their clan with natural resources.

**Warriors**

Responsible for protecting their clan, participating in military engagements, defending territory and carrying out offensive operations.

**Gatherers**

Responsible for collecting natural materials, food, medicinal resources and other useful materials.

**Craftspeople**

Responsible for producing equipment, maintaining settlements and creating tools, weapons and other items.

## 2.3 Independent Affiliations

Additional affiliations may be introduced as the community develops.

**Rogues**

Individuals who operate independently of established faction authority. This may include deserters, former faction members, outcasts or individuals pursuing their own objectives.

**Lone Warriors**

Na'vi who operate outside the authority of established clans. They may retain cultural ties while following independent objectives.

These affiliations should remain relatively limited in population to preserve the importance of the primary faction conflict.

## 2.4 Loreteam

The Loreteam is responsible for the narrative direction and progression structure of the experience.

Its responsibilities include:

- Creating and maintaining the overarching narrative.
- Organising and overseeing official PD events.
- Designing faction Trials and progression requirements.
- Determining faction ranks and approving rank advancements.
- Determining weapon kit eligibility and assigning weapon kits.
- Adjusting, revoking or reassigning ranks and weapon kits whenever necessary.
- Reviewing player-generated lore and significant character developments.
- Maintaining continuity between major events.
- Recognising significant player achievements and contributions.
- Establishing event objectives, ceremonies, hunts, operations and other narrative activities.

The Loreteam has exclusive authority over faction ranks, Trials and weapon kit progression. These systems are not automatically managed or unlocked by the game.

## 2.5 Moderation Team

Moderators are responsible for maintaining the integrity of the community and ensuring that organised events are conducted fairly.

Responsibilities include:

- Supervising official PD events.
- Confirming and documenting recognised character deaths.
- Enforcing community and roleplay regulations.
- Resolving disputes and investigating reported misconduct.
- Preventing exploits and prohibited behaviour.
- Monitoring trading and investigating suspicious transactions.
- Ensuring that Permanent Death is only applied under officially recognised circumstances.

Moderators do not independently determine faction ranks or weapon kit progression.

## 2.6 Development Team

The Development Team is responsible for the technical implementation and maintenance of the experience.

Responsibilities include:

- Developing and maintaining gameplay systems.
- Implementing combat mechanics and abilities.
- Implementing movement mechanics, including sliding, wall climbing and mounting.
- Creating and maintaining weapons, animations and models.
- Implementing approved weapon variations.
- Developing skins and cosmetic content.
- Managing technical issues and bug reports.
- Reviewing and implementing approved purchases.
- Handling player tickets relating to technical content and cosmetic requests.

The Development Team does not independently grant faction ranks or assign weapon kits.

# 3. Permanent Death Events

## 3.1 General Structure

Permanent Death events are the primary gameplay and narrative periods.

These events provide the environment in which players gather resources, develop their factions, construct facilities, participate in combat, trade equipment and influence the ongoing conflict.

A PD event is officially recognised and overseen by the Loreteam and Moderation Team.

Character death is permanent within the context of the character. Players must create or continue with a different character after their previous character has been permanently killed.

Only deaths occurring under officially recognised PD circumstances count as Permanent Death.

## 3.2 PD Event Phases

PD events can be divided into several phases depending on their objectives and duration.

**Preparation**

Players organise their factions, prepare equipment, coordinate operations and establish objectives.

**Development**

Players gather resources, construct facilities, craft weapons, establish settlements and develop their faction's infrastructure.

**Conflict**

Military engagements, territorial disputes, hunts, raids, challenges and other major conflicts take place.

**Resolution**

The Loreteam and Moderation Team conclude the event, document significant developments and establish the consequences of the event.

These phases are organisational guidelines rather than mandatory restrictions on every event.

## 3.3 Persistent Consequences

Events should have consequences that influence subsequent gameplay and narrative development.

Examples include:

- Territory gained or lost.
- Facilities constructed, damaged or destroyed.
- Resources captured or depleted.
- Weapons and equipment acquired or lost.
- Faction leadership changes.
- Significant character deaths.
- Changes in faction relationships.
- Major narrative developments.

The Loreteam maintains historical records of significant events to preserve continuity.

## 3.4 Permanent Death Confirmation

Permanent Death must be recognised under the official event rules.

The Moderation Team is responsible for confirming deaths, recording the relevant circumstances and ensuring that the death is properly processed.

Once confirmed, the character's progression and possessions are handled according to the Permanent Death rules.

# 4. Faction Progression, Ranks and Trials

## 4.1 General Progression

Faction progression is controlled exclusively by the Loreteam.

Players advance through a predefined sequence of mandatory Trials. These Trials represent a character's development, experience, recognition and standing within their faction.

Progression is not based on automatically accumulating experience points or reaching a numerical threshold.

Players must complete the required Trials in their designated order before progressing to subsequent ranks.

The Loreteam determines whether a player has successfully completed each Trial and whether they are eligible for advancement.

## 4.2 Sequential Trials

Trials are mandatory and must be completed in the established order.

A player cannot skip a Trial simply because they demonstrate the abilities or experience associated with a higher rank.

Trials may vary considerably depending on the faction, rank and intended narrative significance.

Examples include:

- Participating in a cultural ceremony.
- Performing a traditional dance in honour of Eywa.
- Completing a hunting expedition.
- Demonstrating knowledge of faction traditions.
- Participating in a military operation.
- Competing in a supervised pit fight.
- Defeating another player under designated conditions.
- Completing an important faction objective.
- Demonstrating leadership or exceptional judgement.

Early Trials may involve relatively simple cultural or introductory activities. Higher-level Trials may involve combat, leadership, dangerous expeditions or significant narrative accomplishments.

The Loreteam determines the exact requirements, conditions and outcomes of each Trial.

## 4.3 Faction Rank

Faction rank represents a character's overall standing within their faction.

There is one overall progression structure rather than separate ranks for individual weapon types.

For example, a Level 3 Warrior may qualify to use both a Level 3 sword kit and a Level 3 spear kit.

They do not possess separate sword and spear ranks.

Their overall Warrior rank determines their eligibility for the weapon kits they may receive.

## 4.4 Loreteam Authority

The Loreteam has exclusive control over:

- Establishing faction rank structures.
- Creating and modifying Trials.
- Approving Trial completion.
- Granting rank advancements.
- Determining weapon kit eligibility.
- Assigning approved weapon kits.
- Revoking or adjusting ranks.
- Revoking or adjusting weapon kit permissions.

The Loreteam may make these adjustments whenever it considers them necessary.

Rank and kit assignments are administrative decisions rather than automatic game unlocks.

The game should provide the necessary systems for recording and enforcing these decisions, but it should not independently determine a player's rank or eligibility.

## 4.5 Character-Specific Progression

Faction ranks, Trial completion and weapon kit permissions are tied to individual characters.

When a character permanently dies, all character-specific progression is reset.

A newly created character must complete the relevant Trials and earn their own progression.

Previous character achievements do not automatically transfer to a new character.

# 5. Weapon Systems and Weapon Kits

## 5.1 General Structure

Weapon progression is connected to faction progression but remains a separate system for determining which combat movesets a player is authorised to use.

The Loreteam exclusively controls weapon kit eligibility and assignment.

A weapon kit determines the standard combat moveset available to a player when using a particular weapon type.

Weapon kits are assigned according to the player's overall faction rank and completed Trial progression.

## 5.2 Weapon Kit Eligibility

Players may eventually qualify for multiple weapon types.

However, access to additional weapon types requires completing the relevant sequential Trials and receiving Loreteam approval.

A player may therefore possess multiple approved weapon kits at the same overall faction rank.

For example:

A Level 3 Warrior may have:

- Level 3 Sword Kit.
- Level 3 Spear Kit.
- Level 3 Bow Kit.

These are different weapon kits, not separate progression ranks.

The Loreteam determines which kits are approved and assigns them to the player.

## 5.3 Weapon Allocation and Equipping

Only one weapon (tool) can be allocated to a character at a time.

Holding approved kits for several weapon types does not allow a character to carry several weapons. The character must exchange their allocated weapon to use a different one.

Players must switch weapons outside combat.

Weapon switching is prohibited during active engagements to prevent players from changing their combat moveset to gain an immediate advantage.

The game should enforce both restrictions: the one-weapon allocation through the inventory and equipping systems, and the switching restriction through combat-state checks.

## 5.4 Weapon Kit Structure

Each weapon kit contains the standard moveset associated with that weapon type.

The standard combat controls are:

| Input | Function |
|---|---|
| M1 | Primary weapon attack and basic combo |
| Z | Ability 1 |
| X | Ability 2 |
| C | Ability 3 |
| R | Critical attack |
| G | Additional special combat ability, if unlocked |

The M1 combo is consistent across variations of the same weapon type.

For example, all standard swords use the same fundamental sword M1 combo and animations. All standard spears use the same fundamental spear M1 combo and animations.

The Z, X and C abilities are determined by the assigned weapon kit and the player's approved progression level.

## 5.5 Weapon Variations

Individual weapon variations may have different appearances, properties and Critical attacks.

For example, two swords may share the same standard sword M1, Z, X and C abilities while possessing different R Critical attacks.

This allows crafted or specially acquired weapons to feel distinctive without requiring an entirely separate combat system for every weapon variation.

Weapon variations should provide different combat options without making one weapon objectively superior.

Any mechanical differences must be implemented and balanced by the Development Team.

# 6. Combat System

## 6.1 Standard Combat

Combat is built around weapon-specific movesets and a consistent control structure.

Players use their assigned weapon kit to perform basic attacks, abilities and Critical attacks.

The standard combat structure consists of:

- M1 basic attacks.
- Z, X and C weapon abilities.
- R Critical attack.
- G additional special combat ability, where unlocked.

The Development Team is responsible for implementing the combat systems, animations, hit detection, cooldowns and balancing.

## 6.2 Basic Attacks

M1 is the primary attack input.

Each weapon type has its own standard M1 combo and animation sequence.

All variations belonging to the same weapon type share the same fundamental M1 structure.

This keeps weapon identity consistent and prevents every individual weapon variation from requiring a separate basic combat system.

## 6.3 Weapon Abilities

Z, X and C are the three standard abilities provided by a weapon kit.

Their functions depend on the weapon type and approved kit level.

The Loreteam determines which kit a player may receive. The Development Team implements the corresponding moveset.

## 6.4 Critical Attacks

R activates the weapon's Critical attack.

Critical attacks are associated with individual weapon variations rather than being entirely identical across every weapon.

This allows different crafted or acquired weapons to have unique finishing attacks or specialised combat functions.

Critical attacks must be balanced according to their damage, range, speed, cooldown and potential for counterplay.

## 6.5 Additional G Abilities

G abilities are optional additional combat abilities.

They are separate from the standard weapon kit and are not required for a player to use their normal combat moveset.

They may be obtained through:

- Approved purchases or transactions involving the Development Team.
- Lore accomplishments.
- Special events.
- Exceptional community contributions.
- Other specifically approved methods.

Examples include:

**Battle Cry**

A combat ability that produces a battle cry or provides a temporary combat effect.

**Grab**

A close-range ability that allows a player to grab an opponent or initiate a specialised interaction.

**Smoke Grenade**

A throwable device that produces a smoke cloud, obstructing visibility and creating opportunities for repositioning.

**Double-Barrel Shotgun**

A specialised firearm ability capable of delivering an extremely powerful, potentially one-shot attack.

G abilities may have entirely different functions, animations, limitations and combat purposes.

They should be treated as individual abilities rather than another progression tier of the standard weapon kit.

The Development Team is responsible for technical implementation and combat balancing. The Loreteam oversees abilities awarded through narrative accomplishments or special events.

G abilities should have clearly defined mechanics, cooldowns, restrictions and counterplay where appropriate.

## 6.6 Defensive Actions

Every character can defend regardless of weapon kit. Defensive actions are not part of a kit and are not granted by the Loreteam.

| Input | Function |
|---|---|
| F (hold) | Block |
| Q | Dodge |

**Blocking**

- A block absorbs a limited number of hits. Each blocked hit uses up block strength, which recovers over time while not blocking.
- When block strength runs out the block breaks: that hit lands with extra damage and the defender is briefly stunned.
- Raising the block just before a hit lands is a perfect block. The hit is negated and the attacker is briefly stunned.
- Hits from behind go through a block.
- Some attacks, such as grabs, cannot be blocked.

**Dodging**

- A dodge is a short burst of movement with brief invulnerability.
- Dodges have a small number of charges that recharge individually.

**Hit stun**

- A character struck by an unblocked hit cannot act for a short moment, but can still move (see 6A.5).

The Development Team tunes all numbers. Defensive actions must stay readable and counterable.

# 6A. Movement System

## 6A.1 General Structure

Movement is a key factor of the experience. It is a core gameplay system rather than a means of getting from one place to another.

Pandora's environment is vertical and dense: forests, cliffs, canopies, rivers and industrial structures. Movement should let players use that terrain to explore, travel, hunt, ambush, pursue, escape and fight.

Skilled movement should be a meaningful advantage that players develop through practice, while remaining readable and counterable by other players.

## 6A.2 Core Movement Mechanics

The following mechanics are key aspects of the game.

**Sliding**

A low, fast slide entered from sprinting. It carries momentum, can be used to pass under obstacles and gaps, and gains speed down slopes.

**Wall Climbing**

Climbing suitable surfaces such as cliffs, rock faces, trees and structures. It gives access to elevated positions, alternative routes and ambush points.

**Mounting**

Riding creatures or operating vehicles to travel quickly across large distances, carry out patrols, raids and hunts, and take part in mounted engagements.

Additional mechanics may include:

- Sprinting.
- Vaulting and mantling over obstacles and onto ledges.
- Ledge grabbing.
- Wall jumping or wall running between surfaces.
- Jumping between branches and other elevated terrain.
- Swimming.
- Rolling or dodging.

The Development Team determines which additional mechanics are implemented and how they are tuned.

## 6A.3 Faction Movement Identity

Movement may reflect faction identity.

For example, the Na'vi may excel at climbing, jumping and moving through natural terrain and canopies, while the RDA may rely more on vehicles, equipment and prepared routes.

Any faction differences must preserve the overall balance between factions and be implemented and balanced by the Development Team.

## 6A.4 Mounts

Mounts include creatures and vehicles that a character can ride or operate.

Mount access, ownership and acquisition must be defined before implementation (see Section 19). Where mounts are earned through narrative accomplishments, the Loreteam oversees the award and the Development Team handles the implementation.

Mounting and dismounting should be clearly readable to other players and should not be instant escapes from combat.

## 6A.5 Movement and Combat

Movement and combat are designed to work together.

- Movement can be used to engage, disengage, reposition, flank and dodge attacks.
- The game must define which combat actions are available while sliding, climbing or mounted, and whether any movement-specific attacks exist.
- Combat effects such as stuns, grabs and roots prevent or interrupt movement mechanics.
- Being in combat does not prevent movement. The combat tag only restricts weapon switching and similar loadout changes.
- Movement mechanics may be limited by stamina, cooldowns or terrain to prevent constant, uncounterable movement.

## 6A.6 Movement Validation

Movement is simulated on the player's client for responsiveness, so the server must validate it to prevent exploits.

The game should verify that:

- Movement speed, sliding speed and jump distance stay within allowed limits.
- Players only climb surfaces that are climbable.
- Players cannot pass through walls, terrain or other solid objects.
- Mounts are only used by characters permitted to use them.
- Movement restrictions caused by combat effects are respected.

# 7. Weapon Skins and Cosmetic Content

## 7.1 General Structure

Weapon skins are cosmetic variations that change the appearance of an existing weapon.

They do not provide additional combat advantages, damage bonuses or access to weapon kits.

A skin does not independently grant permission to use the weapon type it represents.

## 7.2 Acquisition

Players obtain skins through direct contact with the Development Team.

Players may submit tickets to discuss the creation or acquisition of a particular skin.

The general process is:

1. The player submits a skin request.
2. The player discusses the desired appearance with the Development Team.
3. The Development Team confirms whether the skin is available and technically feasible.
4. The price or acquisition conditions are negotiated.
5. The request receives final Development Team approval.
6. The skin is created, implemented or assigned to the player's account.

The Development Team retains final approval over cosmetic requests.

## 7.3 Permanent Ownership

Weapon skins are account-wide cosmetic ownership.

Unlike character-specific equipment, weapon skins are retained after Permanent Death.

Weapon skins cannot be traded or transferred to other players.

They are the only guaranteed permanent exception to the character progression and ownership reset associated with Permanent Death.

# 8. Items, Inventory and Trading

## 8.1 General Item Ownership

Items are part of the game's resource and equipment economy.

Players may obtain items through gathering, crafting, trading, exploration, faction activities and other approved gameplay systems.

Items may include:

- Weapons.
- Crafting materials.
- Consumables.
- Equipment.
- Resources.
- Special items.
- Event rewards.
- Other approved objects.

Items should have clearly defined properties, including their name, description, category, ownership restrictions and tradeability.

## 8.2 Tradeability Rules

All items are tradeable by default, except for:

1. Weapon skins.
2. Items explicitly marked `Untradeable`.

Items marked `Untradeable` cannot be transferred through the normal trading system.

Weapon skins are permanently bound to the player's account.

The `Untradeable` tag should be applied to specific items where necessary rather than making entire item categories untradeable without a defined reason.

## 8.3 Trading Restrictions

Trading is only permitted during active PD events.

Players cannot trade outside an officially active PD event.

The trading system should prevent transactions from being initiated or completed when a PD is not active.

This restriction applies to all player-to-player trading.

## 8.4 Trading System

The trading interface should allow players to initiate exchanges with one another.

A standard transaction should include:

1. A player sends a trade request.
2. The receiving player accepts or declines.
3. Both players enter the trading interface.
4. Each player selects the items they intend to exchange.
5. Both players review the proposed transaction.
6. Both players confirm the trade.
7. A short confirmation countdown begins.
8. Either player may cancel before completion.
9. Once the countdown finishes, the transaction is processed.

The system must verify item ownership and tradeability before completing a transaction.

Items must not be duplicated, transferred twice or removed incorrectly during interrupted transactions.

## 8.5 Trading Records

Trading records should be maintained to assist moderators with disputes and investigations.

Records should include:

- Participating players.
- Items transferred.
- Transaction time.
- Whether the transaction was completed or cancelled.

The system should also prevent players from trading items they do not own or transferring items marked `Untradeable`.

# 9. Permanent Death and Death Satchels

## 9.1 Character Death

When a player permanently dies during an officially recognised PD event, their character is permanently lost.

The player must create or use a different character.

The following character-specific progression is reset:

- Faction rank.
- Completed Trial progression.
- Weapon kit permissions.
- G abilities.
- Character-specific equipment.
- Character-specific possessions.
- Other character-bound progression.

Weapon skins remain permanently owned by the player's account.

No previous faction rank or weapon kit permissions automatically transfer to the new character.

## 9.2 Death Satchels

When a character dies during a PD event, their tradeable items are placed into a death satchel.

The satchel represents the possessions left behind by the deceased character.

Other players may collect items from the satchel during the same active PD event.

### Death Satchel Rules

- All tradeable items held by the deceased character are placed into the satchel.
- Weapon skins are excluded.
- Items marked `Untradeable` are excluded.
- Other players may collect the contents during the active PD.
- Satchels are only accessible during the PD event in which the death occurred.
- Once the PD ends, all remaining satchels and unclaimed contents are removed.
- Death satchels cannot be accessed or looted outside their associated PD event.

Death satchels create an additional consequence for combat and encourage players to consider the value of the equipment they carry.

## 9.3 Storage and Item Security

The inventory system should distinguish between character-held possessions and any separately implemented storage systems.

The exact handling of stored items upon Permanent Death must be defined alongside the storage system before implementation.

The game must not accidentally preserve character-bound items or progression that should be lost upon death.

# 10. Resource Gathering and Economy

## 10.1 General Economy

The resource economy encourages cooperation, faction development and competition over valuable locations.

Players gather materials to support crafting, construction, equipment production and other faction activities.

Resources may be obtained through:

- Gathering.
- Hunting.
- Exploration.
- Resource extraction.
- Faction operations.
- Trading.
- Capturing enemy supplies.
- Event rewards.

Resources should have varying levels of availability and importance.

## 10.2 Resource Categories

**Common Materials**

Frequently available materials used for basic crafting, construction and everyday activities.

**Industrial Materials**

Materials associated with machinery, manufacturing, engineering and technological infrastructure.

**Natural Materials**

Materials obtained from Pandora's environment, including plants, animal products and other naturally occurring resources.

**Rare Materials**

Scarce resources used for specialised equipment, advanced crafting and important faction projects.

## 10.3 Resource Scarcity

Resources should not be infinitely available in every location.

Scarcity encourages players to explore, coordinate gathering operations and compete over important resource locations.

Resource distribution may change between PD events according to the Loreteam's narrative decisions and the Development Team's implemented systems.

## 10.4 Resource Loss and Capture

Resources may be lost through Permanent Death, theft, raids, territorial conflict or the destruction and capture of facilities.

This creates meaningful consequences for faction operations and encourages players to protect their supplies.

# 11. Crafting and Equipment Production

## 11.1 General Structure

Crafting allows players and factions to convert gathered resources into usable equipment and infrastructure.

The system should support cooperation and give materials a practical purpose beyond simple collection.

## 11.2 Weapon Crafting

Players may use appropriate resources and crafting facilities to produce weapon variations.

Crafted weapons may possess unique appearances and individual Critical attacks.

However, crafting a weapon does not automatically grant access to its weapon type or corresponding moveset.

Weapon kit eligibility remains exclusively controlled by the Loreteam.

A player must possess the appropriate Loreteam-approved weapon kit before using the associated combat moveset.

## 11.3 Crafting Requirements

Crafting should require the appropriate combination of:

- Materials.
- Crafting facilities.
- Required permissions or faction approval, where applicable.
- Appropriate character progression, where relevant.

The Development Team determines the technical requirements and crafting interface.

Faction leadership and the Loreteam may establish narrative or organisational restrictions on specific equipment.

# 12. Facilities and Infrastructure

## 12.1 General Structure

Facilities allow factions to establish a physical presence, organise operations and support their members.

Construction requires appropriate materials, cooperation and access to suitable locations.

Facilities may provide practical functions rather than existing purely as decorative structures.

## 12.2 RDA Facilities

**Forward Operating Bases**

Military installations used to coordinate operations and establish a presence in contested territory.

**Resource Extraction Stations**

Facilities used to gather, process and store valuable resources.

**Weapon Manufacturing Facilities**

Locations used to produce weapons and military equipment.

**Medical Stations**

Facilities used to treat injured personnel and support medical operations.

**Research Laboratories**

Locations used for scientific research and technological development.

**Communications Facilities**

Installations used to coordinate personnel and transmit operational information.

**Defensive Outposts**

Smaller military positions used to monitor and defend important areas.

**Storage and Logistics Facilities**

Locations used to organise supplies, materials and equipment.

## 12.3 Na'vi Facilities

**Clan Settlements**

Primary residential and organisational locations for established clans.

**Hunting Camps**

Temporary or permanent locations used to coordinate hunting activities.

**Gathering Areas**

Locations used to collect and organise natural resources.

**Weapon Crafting Stations**

Areas used to create and maintain traditional weapons.

**Defensive Positions**

Locations used to protect important territory and settlements.

**Medical and Healing Areas**

Locations used to treat injured clan members.

**Spiritual and Cultural Sites**

Important locations used for ceremonies, traditions and activities associated with Eywa.

**Resource Storage**

Locations used to organise and protect gathered materials.

## 12.4 Facility Destruction and Capture

Facilities may be damaged, destroyed or captured during officially recognised conflicts.

Their loss should have meaningful consequences for the faction that owns them.

The Loreteam determines the narrative consequences of major facility losses, while the Development Team implements the corresponding gameplay systems.

# 13. Territory and Entitlements

## 13.1 Territorial Control

Territory is an important part of faction warfare.

Locations may provide access to resources, strategic advantages, facilities or important narrative landmarks.

Control over territory can change through military operations, organised conflicts and Loreteam-recognised outcomes.

## 13.2 Entitlements

Entitlements represent recognised authority, ownership or privileges associated with particular locations, roles or organisational responsibilities.

Examples include:

- Leadership positions.
- Control over facilities.
- Access to important resources.
- Authority over particular operations.
- Recognition as a faction representative.
- Control over specific territorial locations.

## 13.3 Challenges

Players and factions may challenge existing entitlements through approved methods.

Challenges may involve:

- Combat.
- Resource contributions.
- Military operations.
- Political decisions.
- Leadership challenges.
- Special Trials.
- Other Loreteam-approved activities.

The Loreteam determines the conditions under which entitlements may be challenged and recognises the final outcome.

# 14. Events and Community Activities

## 14.1 General Structure

Events provide opportunities for players to participate in activities outside ordinary faction operations.

They may be organised by the Loreteam, Moderation Team or approved community organisers.

## 14.2 Event Types

**Faction Warfare**

Organised conflicts between the RDA and Na'vi.

**Hunting Expeditions**

Activities involving the tracking and hunting of wildlife.

**Pit Fights**

Supervised combat events that may also serve as faction Trials.

**Cultural Ceremonies**

Events involving Na'vi traditions, rituals and spiritual practices.

**Military Operations**

Organised RDA missions involving objectives, logistics, combat and territorial control.

**Resource Expeditions**

Activities focused on collecting valuable materials from specific locations.

**Special Lore Events**

Narrative events designed to introduce major developments, characters, discoveries or conflicts.

## 14.3 Player Contributions

Players should be encouraged to develop their own roleplay scenarios, relationships and smaller activities.

However, major narrative developments and changes to established lore require Loreteam recognition.

This allows players to influence the world without compromising narrative continuity.

# 15. Community Structure and Access

## 15.1 Closed Community

The experience is intended for a closed community rather than unrestricted public access.

Entry may be controlled through applications, invitations or approval processes.

This allows the community to maintain consistent roleplay standards and reduce disruptive behaviour.

## 15.2 Community Standards

Players are expected to:

- Maintain appropriate roleplay standards.
- Respect Permanent Death rules.
- Follow official faction structures.
- Respect Loreteam and Moderation Team decisions within their respective responsibilities.
- Avoid exploiting gameplay systems.
- Avoid disrupting organised events.
- Participate in a manner consistent with the experience's intended setting.

## 15.3 Faction Population Balance

The community should maintain reasonable population balance between the RDA and Na'vi.

The Loreteam and Moderation Team may introduce recruitment restrictions or other administrative measures when necessary.

# 16. Development and Administrative Requirements

## 16.1 Progression Records

The game should support reliable administrative records for character progression.

Records should include:

- Player identification.
- Character identification.
- Current faction.
- Current faction rank.
- Completed Trials.
- Approved weapon kits.
- Approved G abilities.
- Character-specific progression.
- Character death status.

The Loreteam must be able to review and update these records.

## 16.2 Item Records

The inventory system should record:

- Item ownership.
- Item category.
- Item tradeability.
- `Untradeable` status.
- Weapon skin ownership.
- Item transfers.
- Death satchel contents.

## 16.3 Permanent Death Processing

Permanent Death should trigger a controlled sequence of actions.

1. The Moderation Team confirms the recognised death.
2. The character is marked as permanently dead.
3. Character-specific progression is reset.
4. Tradeable possessions are transferred into a death satchel.
5. Weapon skins remain attached to the player's account.
6. The character's historical record is retained.
7. The player can proceed with a new character.

The system should process these actions reliably and prevent duplicated or incorrectly preserved items.

## 16.4 Trading Validation

The trading system must verify:

- Both players are participating in an active PD.
- Both players own the items being offered.
- Offered items are tradeable.
- Neither player has cancelled the transaction.
- Items are not transferred more than once.
- The transaction is completed atomically.

Trading records should be accessible to authorised moderators.

## 16.5 Combat Validation

Combat systems should enforce weapon kit permissions and prevent unauthorised weapon use.

The game should verify that:

- The player has been assigned the relevant weapon kit.
- The weapon type is approved for the character.
- Only one weapon is allocated to the character and equipped.
- Weapon switching is unavailable during combat.
- The selected weapon variation is valid.
- G abilities are properly assigned.
- Cooldowns and other combat restrictions are enforced.

## 16.6 Cosmetic Ownership

Weapon skins should be stored independently of character progression.

Permanent Death must not remove account-wide weapon skin ownership.

Skins must not bypass weapon kit eligibility or provide combat advantages.

## 16.7 Movement Validation

The server should validate movement as described in Section 6A.6, including speed limits, climbable surfaces, collision, mount permissions and movement restrictions from combat effects.

# 18. Core Rules Summary

The following rules define the most important gameplay and administrative restrictions.

### Progression
- Faction ranks are controlled exclusively by the Loreteam.
- Weapon kit eligibility and assignment are controlled exclusively by the Loreteam.
- Trials are mandatory and sequential.
- There is one overall faction/Warrior rank, not separate ranks for individual weapon types.
- The Loreteam may adjust or revoke ranks and kits whenever necessary.
- Progression is character-specific.

### Weapons and Combat
- Only one weapon (tool) can be allocated to a character at a time.
- Weapon switching is only permitted outside combat.
- Each weapon type has a consistent fundamental M1 combo.
- Z, X and C abilities are determined by the assigned weapon kit.
- R activates the weapon variation's Critical attack.
- G provides an optional additional combat ability.
- Weapon variations may have unique Critical attacks without changing the fundamental M1 or standard kit structure.

### Movement
- Movement is a key factor of the game.
- Sliding, wall climbing and mounting are core mechanics.
- Movement works together with combat. Combat effects can restrict movement, but being in combat does not.
- Movement is validated by the server to prevent exploits.

### Permanent Death
- Permanent Death resets all character-specific progression.
- Weapon skins are retained permanently.
- Previous faction ranks and weapon kit permissions do not transfer to a new character.
- Tradeable possessions are placed into a death satchel upon confirmed PD death.

### Trading and Items
- Trading is only permitted during active PD events.
- All items are tradeable unless they are weapon skins or explicitly tagged `Untradeable`.
- Weapon skins cannot be traded.
- Unclaimed death satchel contents disappear when their associated PD ends.
- Death satchels can only be looted during the PD in which the character died.

### G Abilities
- G abilities are optional additional combat abilities.
- They are separate from standard weapon kits.
- They may be obtained through approved purchases, lore accomplishments, special events or exceptional contributions.
- The Development Team handles implementation and balancing, with Loreteam involvement for narrative awards.

### Cosmetic Ownership
- Weapon skins are account-wide.
- Weapon skins survive Permanent Death.
- Weapon skins cannot be traded.
- Skins do not provide combat advantages or unlock weapon kits.

# 19. Outstanding Design Decisions

The following details should be finalised before the relevant systems are implemented.

1. **Inventory and storage on death:** Confirm whether separately stored items are protected from Permanent Death or included in the death satchel. The death satchel currently covers tradeable items held by the deceased character.
2. **Death satchel interaction:** Determine whether multiple players can loot simultaneously, whether allies can recover a satchel and how the interface handles competing collection attempts.
3. **Trading restrictions:** Define whether trading is permitted during active combat and whether opposing factions can trade.
4. **Trading confirmation:** Finalise the confirmation countdown, cancellation rules and transaction logging.
5. **Combat switching restrictions:** Define the exact combat-tag duration and which character states prevent weapon switching.
6. **G ability limitations:** Establish individual cooldowns, damage, range, ammunition, duration and counterplay for each ability.
7. **Character storage:** Establish how account-wide skins, character inventories and any personal storage are separated in the data system.
8. **PD boundaries:** Define precisely when a PD begins, pauses and concludes, since trading and death satchel access depend on the event being active.
9. **Movement set and tuning:** Confirm which mechanics beyond sliding, wall climbing and mounting are included, whether movement uses stamina, and which surfaces are climbable.
10. **Combat while moving:** Define which combat actions can be used while sliding, climbing or mounted, and whether any movement-specific attacks exist.
11. **Mounts:** Define which creatures and vehicles exist, how they are obtained and owned, whether they are faction-specific, whether they can be injured or killed, and what happens to them on Permanent Death.
12. **Faction movement differences:** Decide whether factions differ in movement ability and how that is balanced.

These decisions should be documented before implementation so that the game's progression, economy, movement and Permanent Death systems operate consistently.

# 20. Overall Design Objective

The objective is to create a persistent, community-driven roleplay environment where character development, faction identity and player decisions have meaningful consequences.

The experience should avoid becoming a conventional competitive PvP game with superficial roleplay elements. Combat, movement, crafting, progression and resource gathering should all support the broader faction conflict and community narrative.

Permanent Death creates stakes. Sequential Trials establish meaningful character progression. Loreteam-controlled ranks and weapon kits maintain narrative authority. Movement makes traversing and fighting across Pandora a skill in its own right. Trading and death satchels create an economy in which equipment has genuine value. Faction infrastructure gives players shared objectives beyond individual combat.

Ultimately, the game should develop through the actions of its community, with the Loreteam maintaining narrative direction, the Moderation Team protecting the integrity of events and the Development Team providing the technical systems required to support the experience.
